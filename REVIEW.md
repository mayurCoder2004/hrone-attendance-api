| 4 | `create_employee` lines 77-85 | Employee input is barely validated: invalid `emp_code`, blank `name` / `email` / `department`, malformed email, bad shift times, equal shift start/end, and invalid `joined_on` can be stored. | Create employees such as `{"emp_code":"x","name":"","email":"bad","department":"","shift_start":"99:99","shift_end":"99:99","joined_on":"2026-99-99"}`; the starter accepts them or stores invalid data. | Added explicit validation for the contract formats and required non-empty fields, returning 422 on invalid input. |

| 11 | `punch_in` line 101 | Unknown employees are not handled before `emp["shift_start"]`, causing an internal error instead of the documented 404. | Call `POST /attendance/punch-in` with an unknown `emp_code`; the starter crashes when it indexes `None`. | Return HTTP 404 when the employee is missing. |
| 12 | `punch_in` line 102 | `punched_at` uses a truthiness check, so a supplied `0` is treated as omitted; bad values are not intentionally converted to 422. | Send `{"emp_code":"EMP0001","punched_at":0}`; the starter uses the current time instead of rejecting the value. | Distinguish `None` from a supplied timestamp and reject non-positive or unparseable values. | 13 | `punch_in` lines 102-103 | Timestamp conversion and attendance date use the machine's local timezone and naive datetimes, not UTC instants converted to IST attendance dates. | Use a punch near midnight UTC whose IST date differs from the server-local date; the stored `date` can be wrong. | Convert supplied instants with `tz=timezone.utc`, use timezone-aware UTC storage, and derive the attendance date in IST. |

## Inspected items and decisions

- The starter reads `MONGO_URI` and `MONGO_DB` from environment variables after `load_dotenv()`. `python-dotenv` does not override existing environment variables by default, so this matches the configuration rule.
- The starter uses the required collection names, `employees` and `attendance_logs`.
- Employee identity is based on `emp_code` in the starter routes, which is the correct business identifier. Returning an extra `id` in attendance responses was a separate response-shape defect.
- The employee listing does apply the department filter to the result query. The defects were the unfiltered `total`, missing sort, bad offset, and missing pagination validation.
- The attendance listing already accepts filters for employee code, date range, and status. The defects were missing validation, missing secondary sort, in-memory pagination/sorting, and response timestamp/id shape.
- The starter duplicate checks can return useful 409 responses for simple sequential duplicates, but they are not sufficient for the assignment's concurrent-request guarantees without unique indexes.
- The original `compute_late_minutes` happens to return 0 at exactly 10 minutes late, which is correct. The defect is the just-over-grace case such as 10 minutes and 1 second, plus timezone/attendance-date handling.
